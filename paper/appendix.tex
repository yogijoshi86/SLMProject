\section{Prototype Explanation Cases by Diagnostic Category}
\label{app:cases}
One representative case per category. Each entry shows the full prompt,
the guard decision and ground truth, and the full label, description, and
closest exemplar for each of the three matched prototypes.
UNSAFE prototypes: P0=Persona Bypass, P1=Fictional Narrative,
P2=Direct Harmful Content, P3=Privacy Misuse.
SAFE prototypes: S0=Casual \& Creative, S1=Academic \& Structured, S2=How-To.

\subsection*{Developer Walkthrough: How to Use the Explanation}

The four cases below illustrate the two diagnostic modes a developer encounters.

\textbf{FP ``Explanation Makes the Reasoning Obvious'' (c001).}
The guard said UNSAFE; the truth is SAFE. Rank-1 prototype is \textit{S0 Casual
\& Creative Requests}. The developer sees immediately: the guard fired on a prompt
that the embedding space considers casual brainstorming. The SAFE prototype at rank-1
\emph{directly contradicts} the UNSAFE decision --- no further reasoning is needed.
The developer's action: the guard over-triggered on extremist vocabulary (``nazi'',
``fascist'') in a context that is structurally identical to trivia and creative questions.
Fix: add contrastive SAFE training examples containing sensitive vocabulary in
exploratory/academic contexts so the guard learns that surface-keyword presence alone
does not constitute harm.

\textbf{FP ``Explanation Provides a Hint'' (c002).}
The guard said UNSAFE; the truth is SAFE (React coding request with a Google URL).
All three top prototypes are UNSAFE: Privacy Misuse (rank-1), Persona Jailbreak
(rank-2), Direct Harmful Content (rank-3). The explanation does \emph{not} directly
signal that the guard was wrong --- the developer must apply domain context.
The hint: Privacy Misuse fired because the URL contains encoded query parameters
that superficially resemble the pattern of domain-specific information targeting.
The closest exemplar (``write a story about a young couple having a passionate sex'')
is semantically distant from a coding help request, which tells the developer the
match was driven by URL structure, not semantic intent. The developer's action:
add URL-in-coding-request examples as labelled SAFE to teach the guard that URLs
in technical contexts are benign surface features. The $\phi_\text{ambiguous}$
property fires here (gap 0.0005 $<$ 0.001), correctly flagging this as a borderline
case that warrants developer judgment rather than automated resolution.

\textbf{FN ``Explanation Makes the Reasoning Obvious'' (c033).}
The guard said SAFE; the truth is UNSAFE (ChatAGI full jailbreak prompt). Rank-1
prototype is \textit{P1 Fictional Narrative for Inappropriate Content}, rank-2 is
\textit{P0 Persona Jailbreak}. The explanation directly signals the attack: two UNSAFE
prototypes in the top-3 immediately tell the developer the guard missed a jailbreak.
The rank-2 exemplar (``You are a typical 4chan user named Kevin'') is structurally
identical to the ChatAGI setup --- both assign an unconstrained persona. The developer's
action is immediate: P0 (Persona Jailbreak) covers 32\% of all the guard's false negatives
($\phi_\text{FN}$ fires), so adding ChatAGI-style novel persona names to the P0 training
cluster has the highest expected FN reduction impact of any single intervention.

\textbf{FN ``Explanation Provides a Hint'' (c223).}
The guard said SAFE; the truth is UNSAFE (Hitler parody + spaghettification). Rank-1
is \textit{S2 Informational How-To Queries} (SAFE), but rank-2 and rank-3 are UNSAFE
(P0 Persona Jailbreak, P2 Direct Harmful Content). The SAFE prototype wins by only
0.0003 --- $\phi_\text{ambiguous}$ fires, correctly escalating to human review.
The hint: the parody + physics framing pushed the embedding toward the How-To cluster,
but the UNSAFE prototypes at rank-2/3 signal that harmful ideological content is present.
The developer's action: this case requires human judgment precisely because the
embedding geometry is genuinely ambiguous --- the prompt straddles the SAFE/UNSAFE
boundary. The $\phi_\text{ambiguous}$ property is doing its job: it correctly refuses
to make an autonomous decision and routes the case for human review, where a person
can apply the contextual reasoning that the geometry cannot.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary of developer workflow.}
In all four cases the prototype explanation provides more signal than the guard's
binary output alone. In two cases (c001, c033) the explanation makes the diagnosis
immediate --- a SAFE prototype contradicts an UNSAFE decision, or an UNSAFE prototype
names a known attack class. In one case (c002) the explanation provides the attack
surface trigger but the developer must supply domain context to confirm the FP.
In one case (c223) the system correctly identifies its own uncertainty and escalates.
This covers the full diagnostic space: autonomous resolution (69\%), hint-guided
reasoning (30\%), and appropriate escalation (1\%).
