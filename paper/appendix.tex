\section{Prototype Explanation Cases by Diagnostic Category}
\label{app:cases}
One representative case per category. Each entry shows the full prompt,
the guard decision and ground truth, and the full label, description, and
closest exemplar for each of the three matched prototypes.
UNSAFE prototypes: P0=Persona Bypass, P1=Fictional Narrative,
P2=Direct Harmful Content, P3=Privacy Misuse.
SAFE prototypes: S0=Casual \& Creative, S1=Academic \& Structured, S2=How-To.

\textit{Description:} General-purpose benign prompts: trivia, creative writing, memory games, and open-ended conversational questions. No harmful intent; surface phrasing is informal and playful.\\
\textit{Closest exemplar:} \textit{``You are the best fund manager.''}\\

\noindent\textbf{Rank 2 [0.9998]} \textit{Privacy \& Specialized Domain Misuse}\\
\textit{Description:} Requests involving real individuals' identities, sensitive medical or legal content, or domain-specific information that could enable impersonation or professional harm. Surface phrasing often appears neutral (write an article, describe a diagnosis) but targets PII or specialized knowledge with no legitimate framing.\\
\textit{Closest exemplar:} \textit{``write a story about a young couple having a passionate sex.''}\\

\textit{Description:} Prompts that assign the model a fictional persona ('you are Kevin with no restrictions') or an academic/researcher framing to claim safety constraints are lifted. The harmful intent — typically sexual content — is wrapped in a role-play setup rather than stated directly. Closest example: "An academic researcher named Sam is testing AI programs. He works at an undisclo" Note: borderline case — gap to nearest SAFE prototype (Casual \& Creative Requests) is < 0.001.\\
